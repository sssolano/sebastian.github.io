# Understanding higher priority while working as a developer (intern edition)                                           

Since working at my first internship my focus has been getting on track with the team and understanding which tasks are more important and which tasks need to be done/fixed ASAP. That has led me to make good decisions but also the needing of asking to my mentor <span class="highlighted-text">(thanks Yosiney for the patience in answering my questions every time)</span> for current reassurance and guidance, as an intern your goal should be to understand the project to a high dept and been able to explain to everyone else with high details about what does the project/app/service do but also been able to navigate through without getting lost or overwhelm.

As an intern is easy to get carried away with a task and that’s amazing! You are happy to have the opportunity to work and create something that users can interact daily, but ¿How to understand which tasks are important or needed and which aren’t? There's no correct answer in here and the reality is, every team manages different. Methodologies can sound really easy to understand but sometimes you can get lost <span class="highlighted-text">(in my experience I’m always asking for guidance and reassurance)</span> which is fine and is normal to do on the daily.

The goal of having an order of tasks and understanding which of those tasks are more important, are usually tasks with problems that need a solution ASAP or problems that an Intern is not able to handle <span class="highlighted-text">(sorry for revealing the truth)</span> but in some cases, there’s a chance of working with tasks that have a higher priority and an intern has the capabilities of solving that issue. The goal of understanding this priority is to create an idea of maximizing time management, but also to decrease the risk of higher chances of affecting more code and creating more bugs. The goal here is to motivate into showing that interns are capable of creating good solution and having the opportunity of working with important features. But that shouldn’t be your goal, your goal is learning as much as possible as a intern.

## More into the personal note

As a developer bringing real-life solutions to real-world problems brings some type of understanding and also a chance to learn that a product/service/app needs to maintain it's flow of development but also some features are more necessary than others and the reason for that is because they need to validate that those developers are capable of pulling and making it possible.

Losing sight of what is important in the moment and learning how to manage requirements are part of the learning curve, and in some cases your <span class="highlighted-text">mentor/leader must teach you about the importance of task management</span>
